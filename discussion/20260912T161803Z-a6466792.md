Design change (2026-09-12): retention is a request, not a config section. Commit ce722128.

Reconsidered after implementation. The retention section and its timer were the one clock the design added, and the wrong kind: a pass that expired nothing left no commit, and now was taken inside logd, so the outcome of a pass depended on something the log did not record. That is the objection to clocks that do not create commits (compaction's tiering, the snapshot switch), and it applied here.

So retention is a session request, and a write: {retain: {now, batch, author, what: [{path, match, age, after}]}}. The caller carries the rules, the time and the author; logd holds no policy and no clock. A pass given a now is a function of the state and the request, reproducible from the log; the commit is the record that it ran; whoever drives it -- verse on its own schedule, an operator by hand, a cron -- sees the commit it caused. The result says the time measured against, the head after the pass, and per rule deleted / unreadable / skipped counts. In a scoped session it deletes in the scope.

Clock caveats, stated in docs/logd/retention.md: the timestamp is the writer's and now the caller's, so skew moves expiry by the skew, and a future-dated record never expires; for day/year limits noise, for a 1h rule real, and the remedy is to send now from the clock the records are stamped with. logd's commit timestamps are never read by a retain, so the two cannot disagree.

Unchanged from the first design: deletes as commits, age from the record, .* / {*} / (*) containers with [*] refused, the precondition, batching, the compaction horizon, load-time validation of the compaction section, d/w/y duration units.

Not done: docd does not route retain and answers unsupported.
