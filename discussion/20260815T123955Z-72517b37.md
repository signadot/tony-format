Found and fixed in 488bfa8. It was not the range predicate and not the tree
ordering -- both were consistent. It was a cached bound that was one element out
of date.

The walk descends by the [min,max] each parent caches for its children. On a leaf
overflow, add() splits the leaf, merge() builds the parent AND READS THE TWO
HALVES' BOUNDS, and only then does the value go into one of them. When the value
is the new maximum, the merged node's cached max is the element before it, and
every ancestor copies that on the way back up, because updateBounds reads a
child's cached bounds rather than its contents.

The measured shape, inserting 1..49 ascending:

    parent N=49 children=2 bounds=[1,48]     <- says the tree ends at 48
      leaf 1..16
      parent N=33 children=2 bounds=[17,48]  <- says this subtree ends at 48
        leaf 17..32
        leaf 33..49                          <- holds 49

A range walk for [49,49] then reads "this subtree ends before the range begins"
at the first parent and skips it. rangeFunc was right, the tree order was right,
and the max was not the max.

49 ascending inserts is the smallest size that shows it: the first split that
leaves the new maximum alone in a fresh leaf. In logd that is a read for exactly
the one commit whose segment is the tree's maximum -- which is a read at commit N
when the newest snapshot is at N-1, and nothing else.

Fixed by recomputing the merged node's bounds after the value lands. Tests check
the invariant the walk rests on (what a parent says about a child is what the
child holds, after every insert) as well as the consequence (a range walk finds
exactly what a scan finds, over ascending, descending and shuffled inserts at
every size around a split boundary). Both fail without the one line.

The 150-seed soak that turned this up now passes, seeds 88 and 122 included.
