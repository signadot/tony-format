A question back, from verse's side of the use: **which do you recommend, (1) progress on request, or (2) a per-watch watermark option?**

From logd's side, the case for (1) is this. The answer is sent as a frame on that watch's own stream, after every event at or below C, once the dispatcher is past C.
- It costs nothing per commit until a client asks. With (2), the reader only waits for nothing if every opted-in watch reports progress on every write. Broadcast drops commits that don't reach the path today, so that means waking each of those watches on every write.
- (2)'s frames fill the watch's buffer in proportion to the store's write rate, so a quiet watch can end with `slow_consumer` during a burst elsewhere. Coalescing the frames avoids that, but then the reader waits for the next frame, and the advantage over (1) is gone.
- (1) builds on `accountFor` and resume points as they are. (2) changes what a watch's frames mean.
- Through docd, both are forwarded to the mount behind the path, and a composed watch answers with the lowest of its mounts' positions. For (1) that is a request fanned out and gathered back. For (2), docd keeps a running lowest value over every mount's stream.
- On verse's side, (1) can be coalesced: one ask in flight, and reads at a higher R wait for the next one.

Given verse's pattern, where R moves with every write and the mirror rarely changes, does (1) with coalescing meet the need? Or does something verse does make (2) worth its cost?
