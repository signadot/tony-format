Not disagreements:
- TRAILING-ON-WIRE (5,890 records): the wire read one complete document and the text after it was junk. The next read would refuse it, which is how a stream of documents behaves.
- About 8,000 comment records: the run parsed without comments while the wire kept them. With ParseComments on, the two agree (`{#\np}`).
- Sparse-array key order: the parser sorts the keys.

**The wire accepts, the parser refuses**
1. Mismatched closers: `{]`, `[}`, `{a:]`, `[:}`.
2. A stray `:` inside brackets (`{:0}` reads as `{0: null}`), and a `,` or `:` after the document (`{0},`, `{}:`).
3. Duplicate empty keys: `{"", ""}` reads as two `""` keys. The wire does catch `{a, a}` and `{a: 1, a: 2}`.
4. A tag on the line after a key-set key: `{a\n!t}` reads as `{a: !t null}` on the wire. The parser refuses it with "key cannot be tagged" but accepts `{a !t}`. This is the key-set twin of the newline case fe9d7a5c made valid for a tag before a closer.
5. A whitespace-only line before or after the document: `" \n[]"` (extraneous indent) and `"{}\n "` (trailing material). The parser is the stricter side here.

**Both accept, with different values**
6. **The parser drops a trailing tag in an array when no comma precedes it:** `[0 !t]`, `[[]!t]`, `[""!t]`, `[1\n !t]` and `[0#\n!#]` all lose the element. It also carries the tag over a comma: `[A\n\n!A,"b"]` parses as `[A, !A b]`, and `[1 !t, !u]` fails with "two tags". The wire reads `[0 !t null]` and `[A !A null b]`. This is parser data loss, and the most numerous real class.
7. Number spelling: `1E5` stays `1e5` in the parser and becomes `100000.0` on the wire. The values are equal. Large integers are refused by both, and long floats round the same way, so this may not be a defect.

**The wire refuses, the parser accepts**
8. Merge keys: `{<<: x}` fails on the wire with "unexpected token type: TMergeKey".
9. A single-quoted key whose value isn't on the same line: `{\n'a':|\n  x\n}` gives "value with no key: a", and `{"0",\n '|':  \n\n4}` gives "int key not in sparse array". `{a: |\n  x\n}` works.
10. Comments: `[[#\n]#\n#\n]` fails on the wire with "two head comments before one value". The parser accepts it.
