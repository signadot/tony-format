CORRECTION, third time, to the reference data above. This one matters more than the other
two because it points the wrong way about where cost is.

I wrote that the ten largest paths hold 30.1% of all segments and every one is a shallow
ancestor, and I offered that as something reads pay for. Reads do not pay for it. Those

Every ancestor segment in this store is Spine. The only non-spine segments held at any
ancestor path are 252, at the root. So the ancestor chain a read steps over is 844,123
segments at verse.github.issue, 830,645 at verse.git.ref, 740,742 at verse.git-issue.issue --
and in each case all but 252 are skippable. The ancestor chain is a constant, not a function
of history.

WHAT THAT MOVES. The shallow-path concentration is an INDEX SIZE property, not a read-cost
property. 1,520,318 of the 4,519,816 segments -- 33.6% -- exist in order to be skipped. Read
the earlier "top ten paths hold 30.1%" line that way, and not as cost on the read path.

It also relocates what a nearer snapshot buys. The distance from a snapshot is irrelevant to
a path nobody wrote in between, so snapshot distance does not bound a read at a path; what it
bounds is the writes to that path. Property 5's value at read time is therefore concentrated
on the few paths that are actually written often, and its breadth is index size.

The 3.24 MB document, the segment and path counts, the snapshot distribution and the
never-run compaction in the comments above are properties of the stored data and stand. The
parts of those comments that named particular functions were about an implementation that is
being replaced, and should be read as evidence for the bound rather than as defects to fix.
