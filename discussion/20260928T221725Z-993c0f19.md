Thanks, that settles it: not a barrier today. A follow-up to choose between two shapes of (c).

**Which do you recommend?**

1. **Progress on request:** the client asks one watch "are you through C?", and the watch answers once it has accounted for everything up to C (dispatcher past C, and its channel drained up to C).
2. **A per-watch watermark option:** a watch opened with an option reports how far it has accounted, as it goes. That could be a watermark on each event and a progress frame when commits pass with nothing for it, or a watermark frame at some cadence. The client then knows "current through C" without asking.

verse's use is the perms-mirror watch. A read served at commit R waits until that watch is current through R. Under steady writes R moves with every write while the mirror rarely changes, so with (2) the reader would usually find the watermark already past R and wait for nothing. With (1) it would ask each time, or coalesce the asks.

Please weigh:
- what each costs logd per commit;
- how each behaves through docd, where it would be forwarded to the mount behind the path;
- which fits the existing contract better (`accountFor`, resume points, `slow_consumer` ends).
