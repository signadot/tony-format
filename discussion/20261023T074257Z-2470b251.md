What verse would ask of a per-path cutoff, worked through for the records in the table above:

**It is what compaction keeps, not what is committed.** Every overwrite is still a commit, pays its apply (m56xswgsh12ks9ravdn0) and counts towards the next snapshot; snapshots come as often as now, and each compaction drops those paths' deltas instead of keeping ~98.5% of the log. Cutting commits stays the writer's job (verse moved the github iod's out loops from 5s to 1m: pass reports ~35/min → 6/min).

**A short cutoff fits only a path nothing reads at a past commit.** Verse reads at a firing's causing commit when the action runs, after a gate, at a hook, at a sequence step, and a gate can wait hours; that is why staging's store-wide cutoff is 1d and cannot be 1h. So, for the table:

| path | short cutoff? | why |
|---|---|---|
| `verse.iods.<iod>.out` (github, slack, linear, git) | yes, ~10m or none past the current value | bookkeeping for `verse iod`; no rule fires on it, nothing reads it as of a past commit |
| `<package>.…<input>~status` | no, 1d | a package's rules read what became of their outbox inputs (the repo-charters reconciler does), so a status change can be a causing commit read after a gate |
| `verse.schedule.<id>` (the standing tick) | no, 1d | a tick fires rules, the same way |

So the knob has to be per path pattern (or per schema node), not per kind of write: two records rewritten at the same rate can need opposite answers. And a path given a short cutoff wants a read past it said, not answered quietly at a nearby snapshot (cpqj2tf6h12kr5jxqxn0), so a rule that does come to read one fails loudly.
