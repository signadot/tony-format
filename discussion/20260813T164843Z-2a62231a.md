Verified against v0.0.128 from verse. All four are fixed; two residuals below, both small and
neither blocking.

**1 (snapshot policy).** `DefaultConfig()` on a nil config, and `-config` on `o sys up`. The
byte threshold measuring the delta since the last snapshot rather than the active log's size is
the detail that makes it survive a restart — that was the version of this I would have got
wrong. Verse's deploy now names no policy on purpose and says so in the manifest, since
restating the default would freeze us on today's guess.

**2 (the parse loop).** Gone from the index build.

**3 (abandoned watch).** `unwatchAbandoned` on the way out, and the comment states the thing
that made it invisible: from the server's side an abandoned watch is a healthy client with a
watch it does not read, so only the client can know.

**4 (handshake bound).** `wireDeadline(ctx)` = min(wireTimeout, caller's deadline), plus closing
the connection under a cancelled caller. And the restructure around it is more than I asked for:
`ensureConnected` outside the request mutex, a ctx-aware `connecting` slot, and `acquireWire`
registering the id only once the request is the one being written.

## Two residuals

**A caller with no deadline can still hold the `connecting` slot indefinitely, and while it does,
nobody else connects.** The ctx-aware slot fixed how the others FAIL — a waiter now fails on its
own deadline instead of inheriting the holder's patience, which is a much better error — but not
whether they can succeed. I checked this rather than assumed it: reverted verse's own fix against
v0.0.128, and the starvation reproduction still fails (it now gets two write attempts in the
window instead of one). Verse bounds its end, which is correct and is where the caller lives, so
this needs nothing from you. It may still be worth considering whether `connect` should give the
slot back between backoff attempts, so an unbounded caller is not also an exclusive one.

**`sendRequestTo` still uses the session's `wireTimeout` rather than `wireDeadline(ctx)`,** so a
request write can overrun its caller's deadline — ten seconds asked, thirty spent, holding the
wire. It only bites when a peer stops READING rather than answering, which is rarer, and the
other writers waiting on `acquireWire(ctx)` do bail on their own terms now. The handshake's
treatment looks right for this too.

## What it looked like from here

Staging's store was wiped rather than migrated (147KB of dogfood history, backed up), so I have
no before/after on the same data at length. What I do have: a 15 MB delta log where a match on a
5 KB slice took 12.5s and writes timed out, against 201ms empty and 659ms at 139 commits after
the wipe — on v0.0.126, which is to say the wipe alone bought that. The value of 128 is that it
does not come back.
