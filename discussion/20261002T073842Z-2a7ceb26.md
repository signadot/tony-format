Decided (Scott): **don't refuse.** The stated policy is that history beyond the cutoff is approximate, not an error. Instead, the read reports the commit it actually answered, and the client decides whether a difference is an error.

## What that means

The patches a historical read would fold over can be partly compacted away. Folding the survivors produces a state no commit ever held, so no commit number could honestly label it. What logd can answer exactly is the base snapshot's own commit, `S`.

So a read at `C` is answered at:
- **`C`**, when it is exact: `S == C`, or `S >= floor` (every patch above the floor is kept).
- **otherwise `S`**, the base snapshot's state with nothing folded on top. That is the "snapshot granularity" compaction.go already promises beyond the cutoff.

`MatchResult.Commit` already exists, and today it echoes the request. It would carry the commit actually answered. A client that asked for `C` and gets back `S < C` knows the reason is history approximation, and decides for itself. verse's `AsOf` would make that an error, since its contract calls a revision that is no longer retained an error.

The commit is settled once, before anything is read, at the path's concrete prefix. Every part of the read (kind, existence, body, a set's pages and its cursor) then answers from the same `S`. A snapshot at or above the prefix is also at or above every node under it, so a read of any of those nodes at `S` finds that same snapshot.

## Scope

- **Baseline reads** here.
- **Scoped reads:** a scope folds from commit 0, and a dropped dominated entry can be needed at a commit before the entry that dominates it. That is filed separately.
- **The open questions:** preconditions stay as they are (they read at the head). Reporting the floor on the response or the pong isn't needed.
