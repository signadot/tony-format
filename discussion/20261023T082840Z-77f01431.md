On the 10:23 comment, from the plan now on branch `issue-r482sz5rh12ksj` (`go-tony/system/logd/storage/docs/path_history_plan.md`, third draft):

**No loud failure: taken.** The draft before this one had built exactly the guard you withdrew — a floor per declaration, with a read at or under it answered at the ceiling — and it is gone. Nothing refuses or moves a read or a watch for a declared drop; the store's two floors do not move; `AnsweredCommit`, `Deltas` and the protocol are untouched. One persisted number remains, the highest commit of any declared drop, read by one thing: the index's proof that a path was never written, which has to be disarmed once a dropped patch has taken its segments with it (f43dnqpkh12kr1kxqxn0), as the baseline floor disarms it today.

**The current value: not taken as the rule, and here is the fork.** What a read folds is the newest surviving statement at or before the commit asked, or the base snapshot's value, and — your objection, which stands — absence when a leaf's first write after the snapshot was dropped. To answer the current value instead, a read at `at` would also walk the entries in `(at, head]` that reach its path, project each and prune it to the declared subtrees, and fold those on top: on staging that is thousands of decodes for a past read of `verse`, paid by every such read in the hottest path the store has, for a leaf that by the rule which got it declared nobody reads that way. And for a duration other than `0`, which rule applies at a given commit needs the commit's age, which the index does not carry.

So the plan states the fold as the contract — a declared leaf read at a past commit shows a state it was in at some commit at or before that one, never promised to be the state at it, and possibly absent where it stood — and lists the current value as open question 1, with that cost, for Scott.

One correction to the 10:23 rule as written: "beyond the duration, the oldest surviving statement" is not what survives. At `0` compaction keeps, per log file, the newest statement of each leaf; the oldest surviving is the previous file's newest, not the current value. Keeping the oldest too would avoid the absent-at-birth case but not absence after a delete and a rebirth inside a dropped run, so it buys a weaker lie, not the truth, and the plan does not add it.

Unchanged from before: the ticks and `~status` records stay undeclared; a declaration at `verse: verse: iods: "*": out:` covers every iod through a `*` segment in `define:`; and a leaf written with a data tag or comment is never dominated by a later plain write, so if `lastPass` is a tagged timestamp the per-pass entry never drops unless the record is written as `!insert` — worth checking on your side.
