Two claims in the filing do not hold. Correcting them here since the decision was made
against them, even though neither changes it.

1. "_ir says values: .[array(ir)] -- RECURSIVE ... a full dump ... IRView is one level
   deep" was offered as evidence of drift. It is not drift. `_ir` describes an IR
   ENCODING written out -- what marshalling an ir.Node gives -- where recursion is
   correct; `!ir` asks a node about its own representation, where one level is correct.
   The operator's own doc comment says exactly this and I read past it: "base.tony's _ir
   describes the first, !ir asks the second." Two different subjects sharing one
   vocabulary, not two definitions of one thing.

2. "a pattern written from either one is wrong against the other" is moot: `_ir` and `ir`
   are skipped by MergeBaseDefinitions --

       // _ir and ir are left out.  They describe the IR encoding of a document in
       // terms of itself, and expansion of a definition reference is eager, so a
       // schema which mentions them expands forever.

   so no schema can reference them and no pattern can be written from `_ir` at all. It is
   a description, and today an unreachable one.

What survives is the gap the decision addresses: `tag` and `comment` are fields ir.Node
serializes and `_ir` did not declare, so the description was short of its subject. Both
are added.

Also noted while there, unfixed and outside this: schema/schema.tony spells the same
definition `ir: [.nullable(_ir)]` -- an array holding a ref -- where base.tony has
`ir: .[nullable(_ir)]`. One of the two is a typo, and nothing reads either.
