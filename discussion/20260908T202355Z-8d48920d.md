    writes.avg         11.8ms       41.2ms        ->  13.2ms

Note it beats v0.0.205, not just v0.0.206: 16x on reads and half the fold. That is the part
worth recording, because it says the defect was NOT introduced between 205 and 206 -- the
byte ceiling shipped with phase 7 and was latent in both. It is a CLIFF: a subtree under
DefaultPathSnapshotBytes is snapshotted normally, and one over it is never snapshotted
again, at all. So the store ran fine until its hot subtrees crossed 1 MiB, and from then on
those paths' folds grew without bound while everything else looked healthy. The wipe-and-
repopulate at 14:39 and the two measurements at different uptimes are consistent with the
store crossing that line in between; nothing in the 205->206 diff touches scheduling.

The fix prices a snapshot by subtree size rather than refusing one for it, so no path is
ever excluded and the fold is always bounded -- the bound is what size buys. The decline
reasons are counted now (snapshots.path.no.*), which is how the next one of these gets
pinned from the admin face instead of from a CPU profile.
