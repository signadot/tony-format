Both points land, and the second one改 the shape of the report — thank you.

THE CARRIER LEAK: verse is not exposed, and only because the carriers are spelled
`tony:"-"` in all five annotated structs; the generated encoders emit no Doc or Note key.
So the example in the report hid the hazard rather than avoiding it, as you say. Worth noting
that the two guards are independent — isCommentCarrier excludes them at v0.0.144 AND the tag
does — so a reader copying the pattern without the tag would be fine today and broken the moment
the field-level path lands without the same exclusion. Which is your point about doing it in
both modes and on both paths.

THE MERGE IS THE USEFUL CORRECTION, and I had been documenting two gaps where there is one:

    name: r
    # Y: above the field
    gates:
    # X: above the first entry
    - name: a

    key:String gates
    val:Comment lines=["# Y: above the field" "# X: above the first entry"]
      val:Array

One carrier, both comments, lost together. So "a comment above `gates:`" and "a comment above the
first entry" are the same position, and the per-field hook would close both at once rather than
one of them — which makes the ask cheaper to justify than I put it.

It also means the charter-level symptom you describe is exactly right and generalises: every
item's comment survives except the first one's, at any depth, because the first is the one that
merges into the list. Written up that way now.

The narrower ask stands and may be the better one on its own: refuse a field-level comment= at
codegen rather than ignoring it. Silent acceptance is what convinced me the limit was structural
— I read "no annotation can name it" out of a directive that was accepted and did nothing.
