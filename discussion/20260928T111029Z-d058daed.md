**Decided (Scott, 2026-09-28):** each change reports one fact: whether the remote holds the commit the change leaves the issue at. It reads `on <remote>` or `local`, not local/pulled. Refs alone cannot tell "pulled" from "made here and already pushed", and a pull that merges leaves a local merge commit that still needs a push. The remote is the watch's pull remote, otherwise origin, otherwise nothing is said.

A push also moves the tracking refs it wrote, as git does for branches, so the marker is correct right after a push. The watch reads the tracking refs, and when a change that was local becomes held by the remote it prints a `pushed to <remote>` line.
