CORRECTION — I overstated this on filing. Re-measured; the mechanism is real but the consequence is much narrower, and one claim was an artifact of my test.

## What I got wrong

"Data that IS retained becomes unreadable through the path index" — not true. I audited every below-root segment reachable from demo.x.hot after a compaction and cross-checked each against the root index:

    deep kp="demo.x.hot" commit=1 A@0    gen=0 -> ok    | entry RETAINED at root, root read ok
    ... commits 1-5 all ok ...
    deep kp="demo.x.hot" commit=6 B@0    gen=0 -> read interrupted by compaction | entry was compacted away (no root segment)
    deep kp="demo.x.hot" commit=7 B@455  gen=0 -> read interrupted by compaction | entry was compacted away (no root segment)
    deep kp="demo.x.hot" commit=8 B@910  gen=0 -> read interrupted by compaction | entry was compacted away (no root segment)

Every below-root segment that fails belongs to an entry the policy deliberately dropped. No retained entry is unreachable. The "SURVIVOR moved -> deep copy unreadable" line in my original filing came from keying a map by EndCommit, where a patch segment and a snapshot segment share a commit; it was comparing two different segments. Withdrawn.

## What is actually wrong

Compaction leaves the below-root copies of DROPPED entries in the index. That turns the designed degradation into a hard error, but only for path-scoped consumers:

- Root-scoped read: the segments are gone, so the entries are simply absent — history degrades to snapshot granularity, exactly as the policy documents.
- Path-scoped read: the segments are still there, so `ReadPatchesInRange(kp, ...)` resolves them and gets `ErrCompactionInterrupted`.

`ErrCompactionInterrupted` documents its remedy as "callers should re-lookup the segment from the index and retry" (dlog.go:16). For these segments the re-lookup returns the same stale segment, so the retry can never succeed. It is permanent, not transient — which is the actual defect: a stale index entry that the recovery contract cannot recover from.

Blast radius is `ReadPatchesInRange` (watch replay / catch-up at a path) and `ListRange` (can list a path whose entries were all compacted away). State reads do not touch below-root segments as of 67220e2.

Severity down to LOW: no data loss, no silent corruption, fail-stop, and it needs compaction enabled (Config.Compaction, opt-in) plus a watcher catching up across a compacted range.

The fix is correspondingly smaller than what I wrote: either drop the below-root copies when compaction drops the root segment, or have the path-scoped consumers treat an unresolvable below-root segment as "history no longer available at this granularity" and fall back, rather than propagating the error. The second is closer to what the policy already promises.

The two "fix directions" in the original filing were aimed at a bigger problem than exists. Direction 1 (walk all index nodes during compaction) is still the tidy version; direction 2 was really a comment about the read path, which is already resolved.
