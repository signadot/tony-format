# the two shapes through the processor, side by side

Two stores, the same ops, the same snapshots -- one keeping the client's patch, one
keeping a lowered delta -- stopped at the first commit where the lowered store's head
and replay disagree, with both stores' entries shown. The head is the same computation
in both (tony.Patch on the stripped patch), so a difference in the REPLAY is a
difference in what the streaming processor was handed.

```
PLAIN stored:  a: b: !logd-patch-root { k0: 9 }
PLAIN head:    a: b: c: k1: 8 k0: 9 k1: nested: 7 x: k0: 5 d: e: ...
PLAIN replay:  a: b: c: k1: 8 k0: 9 k1: nested: 7 x: k0: 5 d: e: ...      agree

LOW   stored:  a: b: k0: 9 k1: nested: 7
LOW   head:    a: b: k0: 9 k1: nested: 7 x: k0: 5 d: e: ...
LOW   replay:  a: b:       k1: nested: 7 x: k0: 5 d: e: ...               k0 GONE
```

and the same shape on another seed:

```
PLAIN stored:  !logd-patch-root { k1: 16 }
PLAIN head:    a: b: k1: 12 k0: 13 k1: 16 k2: nested: 15
PLAIN replay:  a: b: k1: 12 k0: 13 k1: 16 k2: nested: 15                  agree

LOW   stored:  a: b: k1: 12 k0: 13 k1: 16 k2: nested: 15
LOW   head:    a: b: k1: 12 k0: 13 k1: 16 k2: nested: 15
LOW   replay:  a: b: k1: 12 k0: 13        k2: nested: 15                  k1 GONE
```

Two things to see in it.

**The plain entry carries ONE marker at the write's own path and its replay agrees.**
The lowered entry carries markers at the leaves, and the field that goes missing is a
MARKED one -- `a.b.k0` in the first, `a.k1` in the second. The processor is not losing
an arbitrary field; it is losing one the marker names.

**The lowered delta states more of the document than the write did.** `a.b <- {k0: 9}`
is stored as `a: b: {k0: 9, k1: {nested: 7}}` -- the diff of two states, which included
a `k1` the write never touched. That is expected when the head's base and the write
disagree about more than the write, and it is worth knowing the deltas being replayed
are wider than the patches they replace.

## caveat on the second and third seeds

The probe skips a commit whose head was already dropped, so on seeds where an earlier
disagreement dropped and re-seeded the head, the case shown is not the FIRST divergence
and its state is contaminated. Seed 1's case above is clean -- both stores' heads agree
there -- and it is the one to work from.

## where that points

`patches.BuildPatchIndex` keys entries by the PATH of each marked node, and the
processor applies what it finds at those paths. A marker at a leaf names a path the
processor has to place against the base; a marker at the write's own path names one the
base already has, because the write went there. The next question is what the processor
does with a marked leaf whose path the SNAPSHOT's own index does not have a checkpoint
for -- that index is deliberately size-bound, and it is the only thing in the chain
that is neither the head's computation nor the delta's content.
