The title stays literally true in one case and stops mattering: a scoped watch cannot fold
an arbitrary baseline delta into the value it holds, because the scope's writes apply last.
But the footprint says WHERE the scope has stated something, and that decides, with no
entry read, what a commit is to the watch (26df661, watchStream.step): its own scope's
commit steps the held value as a baseline watch steps by baseline's; a baseline commit that
meets no statement of the scope steps too; one under the scope's claim is dropped; and only
a baseline delta that meets a statement of the scope re-reads at the path and diffs -- and
that read now folds the scope's live statements, not its history (9f13add). Replay follows
the same rule. TestScopedStepAgreesWithTheRecompute holds the rule equal to the recompute at
every commit over mixed streams with claims among them; the three cases are each a test.

The cost the issue tracked, a scoped read that could not be shortened: a scoped read at the
root is 35us to 42us across 50..400 scope writes where it was 844us to 7.45ms. The three
directions the issue listed for the record: bounded overlay (tried and deleted), step
baseline and scope separately (unsound), a path-scoped read (built in the rebuild) -- the
answer was none of them; it was dominance, decided at the write and kept by the index. The
per-event numbers on verse's scoped root watch are to be read on staging from the new
watch.scope.step/drop/reread counters.
