## Measured, and both halves come off the list

**Blind spot 2 -- the operator vocabulary -- was the wrong frame.** Under lowering
what is stored is `Diff(before, after)`, and both sides are STATES, which are
op-free. An operator is a way of ARRIVING at a state, not a way of enlarging the
set of states. So what the generator owes is state-space coverage; whether
`!rename` moves or copies is `!rename`'s business, answered by its own tests and
by review of mergeop. Wiring 38 ops into the generator would couple it to
something it does not own. Dropped.

**Blind spot 1 -- an operator tag as data -- works, and is now pinned.** Written
out as `TestOperatorTagAsData` (root package), seven cases the generator cannot
produce: installing a subtree holding a `!delete`, installing a `!let`, installing
a whole patch as a value, changing a value beside a held op, removing one, an
`!insert` as data, and an op changing into another op. All round trip. The
`libdiff.Escape` / unescape pair is exact.

The two incidents cited when this was filed were CONSUMERS of `!raw` -- a read
path in one, the storage injectors in the other -- not `Diff` or `Patch` failing
to escape. Attributed to the wrong place.

## what measuring turned up instead

`Patch` imposes a standard ordering on objects: it merges through a map and
`ir.FromMap` sorts. So

```
a = c: 1  b: 2  a: 3     Patch(a, Diff(a,b))  ->  a: 9  b: 2  c: 1
b = c: 1  b: 2  a: 9
```

and `o patch` reorders a hand-written file's fields. The property test cannot see
it, because `leftover` compares with `Diff`, which matches object fields by name.

Not a defect: there is no sane alternative to imposing one ordering, and a way to
state an order is a question for some vX after v0.1. The comparison is order-blind
on purpose, and `leftover` now says so rather than being silently blind -- a
reader could otherwise mistake the blindness for coverage.

Closed: nothing here blocks the lowering.
